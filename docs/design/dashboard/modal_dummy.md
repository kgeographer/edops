## Timbuktu: environmental profile

### Setting

**Template**
{Place} sits at about {elevation} m above sea level, in {terrain: flat / rolling / hilly / mountainous} country. It lies in the {ecoregion} ecoregion, {one-line gist from the ecoregion summary}. [The {other ecoregion} lies {distance} km to the {direction}.] [Its surrounding basin also takes in parts of {ecoregion 2} and {ecoregion 3}.]

**Timbuktu**
Timbuktu sits at about 260 m above sea level, in flat country. It lies in the Sahelian Acacia savanna ecoregion, a belt of grassland and thorn scrub between the Sahara and the wetter savannas to the south. The Inner Niger Delta flooded savanna lies about 40 km to the south-west.

### Climate

**Template**
Rainfall averages about {precip} mm a year, {mostly in {months} / spread through the year}. Temperatures average {temp} °C, from {coldest month mean} in {month} to {warmest month mean} in {month}. [It is {among the driest / wettest / warmest / coolest} of the {N+1} basins around it.]

**Timbuktu**
Rainfall averages about 200 mm a year, almost all of it between July and September. Temperatures average 29 °C, from about 22 °C in January to 35 °C in May. Its basin is wetter than the desert basins to the north and drier than the delta basins to the south.

### Water

**Template**
{Place} is {distance} km from the {river name / a river} [, which drains about {upstream area} km² above this point]. [Most of the water passing here falls far upstream: the catchment above is {ratio} times the size of the local basin, and {wetter / drier / cooler} than it.] [A much larger river, the {name}, runs {distance} km away.] [Lakes and wetlands cover about {share}% of the basin.] The basin drains {to the sea about {distance} km away / to an inland sink about {distance} km away / nowhere: it is a closed basin}.

**Timbuktu**
Timbuktu is about 13 km from the Niger, which drains about 380,000 km² above this point. Most of the water passing here falls far upstream: the catchment above is about 100 times the size of the local basin and far wetter than it. Seasonal wetlands cover a large share of the basin. The Niger reaches the sea about 1,900 km downstream.

### Land and geology

**Template**

**Timbuktu**
The land is low and nearly level, with mainly sandy soils over unconsolidated sediments.

### Land cover today

**Template**
Today about {share}% of the basin is cropland and {share}% pasture; {forest / shrubland / bare ground} covers most of the rest. [Population density is about {density} per km², {higher / lower} than in most of the basins around it.]

**Timbuktu**
Today cropland covers a small share of the basin, concentrated along the river; sparse shrubland and bare ground cover most of the rest. Population is sparse outside the town and the river corridor.

### Surroundings

**Template**
{Place}'s basin is one of {N+1} that meet here. [Upstream, {neighbour description} drains into it; downstream, it drains into {neighbour description}.] [Across the divide, the basins to the {direction} are {contrast}.] [On most measures the basin resembles its neighbours.] [It stands out for {variable list}.]

**Timbuktu**
Timbuktu's basin is a narrow corridor along the Niger, one of six basins that meet here. Upstream, the Niger arrives from the Inner Delta; downstream, it continues east. Across the divide, the basins to the north are desert, far drier and with almost no cropland or wetland. The basin stands out from all of its neighbours for its wetland and its population.

## Notes for the next step

- The next step would be to take each sentence and ask: can a rule produce it from data we have, does it need a cutoff, or does it need new data (named directions, lithology, monthly temperatures, distance to coast)?
- Some sentences carry verdict words ("stands out", "much larger", "resembles"). Those are the ones that need a cutoff and a sweep.
- The Surroundings section is the hardest. It is also the one the ring was built for.
