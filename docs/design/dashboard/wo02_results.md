## Step 2 summary: what the payloads carry (check-in 2)

**Method.** A 12-cell notebook (`notebooks/edop/dashboard/wo02_step2_field_availability.ipynb`) read the saved payloads only: 537 queries that returned 200 (single basin 428, buffer 104, polity 5; cell 8 also included fixture F34, added during the step). It tabulated every field in cheat sheet 2.3 by scope, level, method and status (matrices saved in `output/edop/dashboard/wo02/step2/`), then compared keys, counts and sizes with the cheat sheet.

**Headline.** The row structure matches the cheat sheet. Every A–E query returns exactly 52 rows with the same 20 keys, no undocumented row key exists, and the 2.6 row-combination counts for Timbuktu single basin and the 100 km buffer match to the row (cell 9). What differs is the behaviour behind the fields, listed below. Detail and evidence are in issues 5 and 6.

**Where the cheat sheet and the payloads disagree** (corrections queued, none made):
- `friendly_name` and `db_col` are absent on all Band T rows; `db_col` is null on the two derived rows (`outlet_type`, `coast_fraction`) of every query (6c, 6d, 6g).
- `weight_at_zero` is computed for every area-weighted row, not only zero-inflated variables, and in a buffer it is a share of the circle, sea included (6a, 6e).
- `modality` reads `unimodal` on rows where the test cannot run; `two_regime` occurs only on buffer rows with data (6b).
- `detail` blocks: `regimes` and `suppressed_score` on `area_weighted` rows too; no histogram on `distribution_only`; undocumented `modal_label`, `perennial`, HYDE `sd` and `w_eff`; extra keys in `detail.distribution` (6h, 6i, 6j).
- `no_data` means "missing" for slope, stream gradient and soil texture but "no wetlands" for `wetland_class` (issue 5).
- The polity top-level keys (`resolver`, `member_ids`, `modality_post_pass`, `band_t_span`) are documented elsewhere in the cheat sheet but missing from the 2.3 list (cell 5).

**What a rule may rely on.** Present on every A–E row: `variable`, `friendly_name`, `band`, `method`, `unit_type`, `n_units`, `status`, `coverage`, `score_suppressed`, `caveat`, `detail`. Scores are null for `no_data`, `outside_active_domain`, `not_areal` and suppressed rows, and by design for categorical and flag rows. A raw value exists for single basin, one-basin buffers, `dominant_basin` and categorical rows. The `modality` and `coherence` fields are meaningful only on multi-basin rows with data. Band T rows have no score, `coherence` or `modality`.

**What the payload cannot give.**
- Which variables are zero-inflated (the catalog holds it).
- Readable names for Band T variables.
- The meaning of `no_data` per variable (the provider's pages hold it).
- Global position for Band T.
- The members' exact scores (only a 20-bin histogram).

**Limits of the evidence.** Band T was harvested for only 6 fixtures (5 single basin, 1 polity); no buffer, L8 or BCE-polity Band T. Polities are 5 queries. The population sample uses equal allocation per climate zone, so its rates are not world rates.

**Proposed for Step 3 (for your decision).** Start with the families that rest on solid ground: position, empty cells, local and upstream, coverage and shortfall. Treat the buffer branch as a separate family, because three of the seven anomalies root there.

## The buffer scope: Karl's position and the evidence as it builds

This section is kept current as each family reports. It states Karl's position and lists the evidence for it, with pointers; it is not a recommendation. Whether the buffer scope stays is Karl's decision (cheat sheet 7.2 item 12).

**Position (Karl).** 2026-10-30: a buffer has no conceptual basis for a terrestrial service; the bound is not justified by any logic of its own. 2026-10-31: buffers are also problematic because they take in water where there are no basins, and the evidence keeps mounting.

**Evidence**
1. *Anomalies.* Of the seven unexpected behaviours in the first field matrix (Step 2 cell 3), three exist because of the buffer scope: the `two_regime` modality call (6b), the one-basin buffer whose statuses change with the sea share (6e) and score suppression that depends on the basin count (6f). A fourth, `weight_at_zero` (6a), becomes a share of the circle, sea included. The other three (`no_data` meanings, `units`, `db_col`) do not depend on scope.
2. *Sea.* A buffer takes in area that belongs to no basin: `shortfall` is 0.50 for Rome at 200 km, 0.75 for the Caspian shore and 0.95 for Shetland. Coverage and the engine's zero guard are measured against the whole circle, so sea changes the engine's own verdicts: six rows of the Shetland buffer carry a different status from the single-basin query for the same basin, and in 19 of the 104 buffers, 119 rows have all of their data-bearing area at zero yet are labelled `ok`, with shortfalls from 0.03 to 0.95 (6e; Position notebook cell 8).
3. *Low coverage is mostly sea.* In the 55 `low_coverage` rows (fixtures and population), 35 are explained by sea alone, 14 by basins without a value alone and 6 by both (Empty-cells notebook cell 8, confirmed).
4. *Small and variable basin counts.* Buffers hold 1 to 17 basins in the population sample (median 10; 7% have fewer than 5) and 55 in the Greenland fixture. At 2 to 4 basins the two-groups call is close to automatic and 57 to 80% of rows are suppressed (6b, 6f); the coherence label has a small-n guard and the modality call does not. A one-basin buffer behaves as a single basin (6e).
5. *Slivers.* A buffer around a point inside a basin can return no basin when that basin is under 0.1% of the circle (Rockall, issue 2).
6. *Coastality.* Handling sea properly would need a coastality measure, which is deferred pending data such as bathymetry (issue 4).

7. *Pairs lose their gap.* Nearly half of the local and upstream pairs in buffers (47%, weighted) have no usable percentile gap, against 25% at single basin L6; suppressed two-group scores take out one or both sides (Local-and-upstream notebook cell 5).

8. *The radius sets the spread.* Because basins within 100 km are far more alike than random basins (observed middle-80% width 5.5 times narrower than random draws), the spread statements for a buffer largely describe the 100 km bound: polities over larger areas are less tight (ratio 0.40 to 0.54). A different radius would change what "concentrated" means (Distribution family, spatial-autocorrelation check).

**Where buffers do better.** At a coastal point a few metres seaward of the simplified coastline, the single-basin and ring queries return a 404 while a buffer still works (issue 1).

**Limit of the evidence.** The population sample holds 100 buffers and only 5 polity queries. Polities also combine many basins, so they are under-examined here; this is not evidence that they are clean.

**If the scope went:** issues 2 and 6f and most of 6e would disappear, issue 4 would lose its urgency, and the buffer branches in the rules (POS-06's coverage division, EMP-05, the basin-count statement) would not be needed.

## Issues found

### 1. Coastal places can fall outside every basin polygon (open, not fixed)

**What happens.** A point a few metres seaward of the simplified coastline is in no L6 or L8 polygon. Single-basin and ring queries return 404 "No basin covers the given point location". A buffer around the same point works, because it only needs an overlap. Oceanic islands are a different case: BasinATLAS has no basins for them at all, so every scope returns a 404.

**Evidence** (database point-in-polygon checks; L6 and L8 give identical results):
- Reykjavik (64.15, −21.94): nearest L6 and L8 polygon is 27 m away. Single basin 404; 100 km buffer returns 8 basins.
- World Heritage Cities, 258 places: 4 outside every polygon. One is a coastline miss (Levuka, Fiji, 4 m); three are oceanic islands (Cidade Velha, Angra do Heroísmo, St. George's, 650 to 1,110 km from the nearest basin).
- GeoNames cities of 15,000 or more people, 27,699 places: 65 outside (0.23%). 8 are within 1 km (Tromsø 115 m, Akureyri 195 m, Knokke-Heist 18 m and others); 57 are more than 10 km away (Honolulu, Port Louis, Saint-Denis, Praia, Malé and other oceanic islands). None fall between 1 and 10 km.

**What the rates do and do not say.** Both samples are curated and small. The World Historical Gazetteer holds millions of place records, so what matters is coastal places in general, not these sets. The percentages show that the effect exists and what causes it; they are not an estimate of how often users will hit it. The frequency across WHG places has not been measured.

**Options for later (nothing decided).**
- Snap a point to the nearest basin within a tolerance. In the GeoNames sample a 1 km tolerance rescues all 8 coastline misses and nothing else; 100 m rescues 1. It does nothing for oceanic islands.
- Separate "just offshore" from "oceanic island, not covered" in the error message, since the current wording is accurate but not informative.
- Relate to the coastality question (issue 4): a rule for calling a place coastal would have to decide how a point just off the coast is treated.

**For WO-02.** The statement rules need an error or not-applicable case for places with no basin, and the coastline and island causes are different. Fixture F31 (open Atlantic) covers the plain no-basin 404.

### 2. A buffer can 404 although the point is inside a basin (minor, open)

Rockall (57.5966, −13.68823): the containing L6 basin is 0.29 km². The buffer resolver drops any basin whose overlap is below 0.1% of the buffer's area (`engine.py:resolve_buffer`, `epsilon=0.001`). A 100 km buffer has about 31,416 km², so the cut-off is about 31 km², and Rockall's basin is far below it. Nothing remains and the endpoint returns 404 "No basins fall within this buffer". Single-basin at the same point works. Tested on five other small islands: those with no polygon at all 404 under any scope (issue 1); none had a polygon in the range below 31 km². This is a data curiosity, not a proposed change. In the population sample it is query P156b, excluded as an expected 404.

### 3. Sandbox coordinate box rejects a typographic minus (open, fix proposed, not applied)

Coordinates containing "−" (U+2212) instead of "-" do not match the coordinate pattern (`sandbox.html`, `resolveSettlement`). The text then goes to the place-name search, where the second number is read as a country; the country does not resolve, so the server falls back to the map's visible bounds as a bounding box, and with the map at world view those bounds are out of range. The user sees "Error: bbox out of range or degenerate", which points nowhere near the cause. Proposed: normalise U+2212 and the en and em dashes to "-" before matching. Separately, the out-of-range bounds fallback is a smaller latent bug (any unresolved country with the map at world view). Neither is changed; WO-02 does not change app behaviour.

### 4. Coastality: data gap and its place in this work (open, deferred)

The coastality measure is deferred because it needs data EDOPS does not have (bathymetry, for one). This matters to WO-02 in three ways, and may be a necessary scope extension:
- Buffers near a coast include sea that belongs to no basin (`shortfall`: 0.50 at Rome 200 km, 0.75 at the Caspian shore (43.0, 51.0) 150 km). The label "R km buffer" promises a full circle while the numbers describe only its land part.
- Issue 1 needs a rule for whether a place is coastal, and what follows for how it is described.
- Karl's view (2026-10-30): a buffer has no conceptual basis for a terrestrial service. Recorded in cheat sheet §7.2 item 12.

### 5. `no_data` means different things for different variables (open)

**What happens.** A row has status `no_data` when the source value is BasinATLAS's −9999 marker. The provider's own catalog pages (in `app/static/basinatlas_pages/`) show that the marker means different things by variable, but the payload and the Sandbox label it the same way (*no data*).

| Variable | What −9999 means (provider page) | L6 basins with −9999 |
|---|---|---|
| `wetland_class` (L05) | "no wetland areas" in the basin: **absent**, not missing | 2,623 (16%) |
| `slope_avg`, `slope_upstream` (P02) | assigned to all of Greenland on purpose, because DEM outliers over the ice sheet gave unreasonable slopes: **missing** | 302 (lat 60.6 to 82.9, lon −68.6 to −18.2) |
| `stream_gradient` (P03) | same Greenland reason: **missing**. The provider page says nothing about the other 110 basins with −9999 (Australia, Brazil, Canada among them) | 412 (302 Greenland, 110 elsewhere) |
| `pct_clay`, `pct_silt`, `pct_sand` and `_upstream` (S01 to S03) | the source grid had no data for the entire basin (mostly deserts and open water): **missing** | 757 (clay) |

All four of wetland class, slope, stream gradient and clay are −9999 together in 155 basins (0.9%).

**Why it matters.**
- The Sandbox shows *no data* for `wetland_class`, which reads as "unknown" when the truth is "no wetlands". Of the app's wordings, *not present here* would fit. `emptyCause` does not distinguish this variable.
- A statement rule for `no_data` needs a per-variable meaning. "No data for this variable" is true for slope and soil texture and misleading for wetland class.
- The catalog's wetland-class rarity figure (0.71% to 49.2% of non-null L8 basins) excludes these basins.

**Seen in the population sample (306 single-basin L6 queries):**
- 106 queries have at least one `no_data` row.
- 40 have all ten variables empty together (wetland class, stream gradient, slope pair, soil texture six). All 40 have −9999 for slope, so they are Greenland basins. This is an artifact of equal allocation per climate zone; the world count is about 155 basins.
- 30 have exactly one `no_data` row: 29 are `wetland_class` (no wetlands) and 1 is `stream_gradient` (cause unexplained, like the 110 above).

**Open.** Why 110 non-Greenland basins have −9999 stream gradient. The provider pages for the other variables with a −9999 marker have not been read.

### 6. Payload behaviours the cheat sheet does not describe (open; cheat sheet corrections queued for after WO-02)

Found while reading the field-availability matrix (Step 2, cell 3) over 537 payloads (all that returned 200: 325 single-basin L6, 103 single-basin L8, 104 buffer, 5 polity). Each item: what the payload does, then what it means for rule-based prose.

**6a. `weight_at_zero`.** The share of the query's area (its weight) in basins at percentile exactly 0.0. The engine computes it for every `area_weighted` and `distribution_only` row that has data (`engine.py`, B1), not only for zero-inflated variables as the cheat sheet's field table suggests. At single basin it is only 0 or 1 (10,563 and 3,715 rows). It was above 0 for 29 variables. In buffers: `outside_active_domain` rows all have a value of 0.90 or more; 996 `ok` rows have 0.5 or more (for example `river_area` in P001b is 0.9988, `gw_table_depth` is 0.9029 with a headline score of 0.69).
- Where it is 1 for a single basin (`ok` status), the raw value is exactly 0 in every case: 17 variables, every row (population density, runoff, groundwater depth, river area, aridity, slope, distance to sink, human footprint, upstream soil texture and others). So percentile 0 corresponds to a true zero for these variables. Buffers carry no raw values, so for them this is an inference.
- These rows are not `outside_active_domain`, which needs the variable to be zero in 20% or more of all basins as well.
- Open: upstream clay, silt and sand are 0 in 22, 18 and 16 single-basin rows. A soil cannot have 0% of all three, so this may be a source artifact. Not investigated.
- *Prose:* the field supports "none of this here" (single basin) and "about N% of this area ranks at the bottom for this variable" (areas). The headline percentile hides it (a mean of 0.69 with 90% of the area at the bottom). Any "most of" wording needs the number beside it and a cutoff sweep.
- **The payload does not say which variables are zero-inflated** (WO constraint 6; Step 2, cell 10). 35 of 52 variables carry a non-null `weight_at_zero` (the 35 `area_weighted` variables, including non-zero-inflated ones such as temperature, elevation and aridity), and `weight_at_zero` is the only key naming "zero" anywhere in the payloads. The engine reads each variable's global zero fraction from the catalog (`zero_fraction_s_L6`, `zero_fraction_s_L8` and the `_u_` columns in `documentation/EDOPS_variable_catalog_v0.4.tsv`, threshold 0.20; `engine.py:load_catalog`, `rank_expr`), not from the payload. *For the master table ("needs new data?"):* a zero-aware branch needs a catalog lookup now, or a payload field later (an engine change, outside WO-02). At single basin it is not needed for "none of this here", since weight at zero 1 means a raw value of 0. 1,474 rows have a score of exactly 0.

**6b. `modality` is a placeholder on many rows.** `unimodal` appears on every single-basin `area_weighted` and `distribution_only` row, including `no_data` (702) and `outside_active_domain` (3,127) rows, and on buffer `no_data` (108) and `outside_active_domain` (440) rows. Polities are null by design. `two_regime` appears only on buffer rows with data (753 `area_weighted`, 75 `distribution_only`). *Prose:* do not read `modality` at single basin or on rows without a score; use `score_suppressed`.
- **How the call is made** (`engine.py:detect_modality`, provisional cutoffs `_MODALITY_GAP = 0.50`, `_MIN_REGIME_WEIGHT = 0.20`): sort the member basins' percentile scores; a gap between neighbours larger than half the p10-to-p90 spread, with at least 20% of the area on each side, gives `two_regime`; otherwise `unimodal`. The field defaults to `unimodal` when the test cannot run (no spread, fewer than 2 values), so `unimodal` means "no split found", not "tested and found to be one group". For a two-regime row the headline score is blanked (kept in `detail.suppressed_score`) and `detail.regimes` holds each group's centre and weight.
- **Two basins (reasoning from the code, not checked row by row).** With 2 basins the gap between them is nearly the whole spread, so the gap test passes whenever the two scores differ; the only remaining requirement is at least 20% of the area on each side. The call at 2 basins is therefore close to automatic, which fits the 70% suppression rate in 6f.
- **Sweep limit.** WO-02's sweep rule asks for cutoff-dependent statements to be re-evaluated over a range of cutoffs. These cutoffs are inside the engine, and the payload carries the verdict plus each group's centre and weight, not the member basins' exact scores. It does carry `detail.distribution` (6i): a 20-bin weighted histogram of the member scores plus exact `p10` and `p90`, which is enough to re-run the test approximately (exact threshold, bin-resolution gaps). The re-test is nearly exact for small n and approximate where a gap is borderline or several basins share a bin.

**6c. `units` follows the raw value, with gaps.** Null for class labels (biome, zone, ecoregions, land cover, lithology, outlet type, wetland class) and for two dimensionless numbers, `human_footprint_09` (with its upstream pair) and `coast_fraction`. Polity `not_areal` rows carry `units` but no raw value. *Prose:* value templates need a no-units form.

**6d. `db_col` is null on exactly two derived rows per query:** `outlet_type` (`class_mixture`) and `coast_fraction` (`flag_fraction`); 536 each over 536 queries with Bands A to E. The cheat sheet calls it the raw DB column without noting this. *Prose:* never depends on it.

**6e. A buffer can be a one-basin result, and the sea changes its verdict.** P069b is a 100 km buffer at (60.24, −1.54), Shetland: one basin (2060059370) touches the circle and `shortfall` is 0.946 (94.6% of the circle is sea). Its 35 `area_weighted` rows carry a raw value and coherence is cleared, as for a single basin (cheat sheet 2.7 says so). It is the only one-basin buffer among the 104 sampled population buffers. Compared with the single-basin query at the same point (P069s), the scores and raw values agree (for example aridity 98.06 and 313 in both), but 49 of the 52 rows differ in other fields:
- `coverage` is 0.0537 on 49 rows in the buffer (the basin is 5.4% of the circle) and 1.0 in the single-basin query.
- Six rows are `outside_active_domain` in the single-basin query but `ok` in the buffer: `karst`, `karst_upstream`, `permafrost_extent`, `dist_sink`, `wet_pct_grp1_upstream`, `wet_pct_grp2_upstream`.
- Nine categorical rows (biome, zone, `eco_id`, both freshwater ecoregion fields, land cover, lithology, `pnv_majority`, `wetland_class`) are `ok` in the single-basin query and `low_coverage` in the buffer (the coverage rule working as designed).
- *Cause of the first difference* (`engine.py`, B1): `weight_at_zero` is summed from the raw weights, so in a buffer it is the share of the whole circle, sea included (0.0537 here, 1.0 for the single basin). The `outside_active_domain` guard fires only at 0.90 or more, so when more than 10% of a buffer is sea the guard cannot fire. A variable that is zero across every land basin then comes back `ok` with percentile 0. Seen on 6 of 52 rows in this one query.
- *Prose:* branch on `n_units`, not on scope alone. In a buffer, `weight_at_zero` is not a share of the land: read `shortfall` and `coverage` first, before any other field. The same place can carry a different status as a buffer than as a single basin. Relates to issues 1 and 4: near a coast the buffer's sea shortfall changes the engine's own verdicts, not only the wording of the heading.
- *Not only Shetland (Step 3, Position notebook cell 8).* Across the 104 buffers (fixtures and population), 119 rows in 19 buffers have all of their data-bearing area at percentile 0 but `weight_at_zero` below 0.90, so the guard did not fire; shortfalls range from 0.03 to 0.95, and the largest groups (11 rows each) are in buffers with shortfalls of 0.12 to 0.34.

The rate depends on how many basins the buffer holds:

| 2 | 37 of 53 |
| 3 | 12 of 15 |

The cheat sheet (2.7) says coherence is cleared below 5 basins; the two-group call is not, and at 2 to 4 basins it is made on 57 to 80% of rows. With 2 basins "two distinct groups" says little more than that the two basins differ. *Prose:* a statement about suppression should print the basin count beside it; no cutoff is set here (WO constraint 2). This rate is for the sample as drawn (buffers at random basin centres), not a world rate.

**6g. Band T rows carry a different field set** (Step 2, cell 4; polity L6 F29 and single-basin L6 F26, F27, F28, F30, F33 only: 213 and 759 rows; no buffer, no L8, no BCE polity).
- `friendly_name` and `db_col` are absent (the keys do not exist) on every Band T row; cheat sheet 2.3 says every row has them. *Prose:* readable names for Band T variables must come from outside the payload (the friendly-name crosswalk is a separate work order).
- `representative_score`, `coherence`, `modality` and `weight_at_zero` are present and always null. The code states "Band T has no global-percentile ranking" and gives no reason; the likely one is that ranking would need a world table per variable per year or epoch over grid cells, which the engine does not build (inference, not documented). *Prose:* no global position can be stated. Other positions are in the payload: LMR air and precipitation are anomalies against each cell's own 850–1850 CE mean (sign and size are statable), HYDE gives totals in km² with a per-cell distribution in `detail`, eVolv2k gives event years and sizes.
- `coverage` is filled for HYDE and LMR and null for eVolv2k (a global series). `epoch_year` is filled only for HYDE rows; `year` is filled on all three sources.
- A span with no volcanic events returns zero eVolv2k rows (F33, 1140–1170: 33 eVolv2k rows over the other four single-basin queries, none from F33). The absence of rows is the signal; there is no zero-valued row. *Prose:* a rule must treat "no eVolv2k rows" as "no events in the span", and must separately know when the span lies outside the series (−491 to 1890 CE).
- **HYDE after 1950 (fixture F34, Timbuktu, 1940–1960).** `hyde_caveat` appears, and only on the 1950 epoch's rows: "HYDE 3.4 cadence-transition artifact: 1950 is the last centennial/decadal epoch before annual data begins; value may not reflect real land-use change." The span yields 12 HYDE epochs per variable (1940, then every year from 1950), so a HYDE row count depends strongly on where the span falls (1 or 2 epochs for earlier spans). `hyde_nearest_year` appears on F28 (BCE span) and F33 (1140–1170), the two spans with no HYDE epoch inside them (8 rows): "No HYDE 3.4 epoch falls within this span; nearest available step shown." `hyde_caveat` appears on F34 only.

**6h. `detail` blocks differ from cheat sheet 2.4 in five ways** (Step 2, cell 6; all 537 payloads with a 200).

### 7. Prose hazards found in the Position draft sentences (Step 3, family 1; open, for the friendly-name crosswalk)

Draft sentences were produced for seven fixtures (notebook `wo02_s3_01_position.ipynb`, cell 4). Two problems cannot be fixed by a rule.

**7a. A variable's name does not say which way it points.** "Aridity index (P/PET) ranks at the 11th percentile among all L6 basins worldwide" is Timbuktu, a dry place, but the index is precipitation divided by potential evaporation, so a low value means dry. A reader meeting "aridity … 11th percentile" can take it to mean "not very arid". The percentile direction is fixed (a higher percentile is a higher value, WO constraint 7); the meaning of high and low for a variable is not in the payload. *Needs:* a per-variable note on what a high value means, in the friendly-name crosswalk work order.

**7b. Raw values and their units read badly as stored.** "Aridity index (P/PET) is 9 P/PET ×100" prints the catalog's units string with the stored value; the reader needs 0.09, or a plain label. Raw values are printed as stored (POS-03 "needs new data": display scale and unit wording per variable are not verified; a few variables are stored with a scale factor). *Needs:* a per-variable display rule (scale and unit wording), also in the crosswalk work order.

**7c. What zero means differs by variable.** A raw 0 is an absence for karst, cropland or wetland extent, sea level for elevation minimum (51 one-basin rows at 0 m have a percentile of about 15, not 0), 0 °C for temperature, and "at the outlet" for flow distance to the marine outlet (33% of L6 basins). A sentence "X is zero here" is literally true in each case but reads as "none" in only some. *Needs:* a per-variable meaning of zero in the crosswalk work order; until then the rules say "zero" and for zero-aware variables "zero for this variable", never "none".

**7d. What area a variable covers is not in the payload (Step 3, Local and upstream).** The payload has 13 local and upstream pairs and 15 continuous variables with a local value only (for example elevation, runoff, groundwater depth, discharge, temperature extremes). A sentence that says such a variable "is given for this basin only" can be wrong: river discharge, for one, may by definition include everything upstream, and the cheat sheet describes band B baselines as covering the full upstream catchment. Which variables cover the sub-basin, which the whole upstream catchment, and which only a point is not stated in the payload. The rules say "there is no separate upstream value" and nothing more. *Needs:* a per-variable note on the area covered, in the friendly-name crosswalk work order.

Two fixes were made in the rules themselves: small values were rounded to a wrong number ("Discharge annual mean is 0 m³/s" for a value of 0.002; now printed to two significant digits), and POS-02 no longer applies when the score is exactly 0, because POS-06 already says that all of the area with data is at the bottom.

### 8. HYDE grazing land is not pasture plus rangeland, in the source cells (open; found during Band T, Step 3)

In the Band T payloads, grazing land equals pasture plus rangeland (within 0.5 km2) in 68 of 75 epochs checked and is larger in the other 7, by up to 56,911 km2 (the BCE polity, 44% of its grazing total). The cell-level check shows this is in the source, not in the aggregation: across all 2,215,829 `temporal.hyde_cells`, with no missing values, grazing exceeds pasture plus rangeland in 225,394 cells at 0 CE, 254,579 at 1000 CE and 336,113 at 1950, and is never smaller. The largest excess in a cell is 85.8 km2, the area of a 5 arc-minute cell at the equator, so some cells are entirely grazing land while pasture and rangeland are both zero. Globally, grazing sums to 1.70 million km2 against 1.31 million for pasture plus rangeland at 0 CE (3.96 against 3.40 at 1000 CE, 24.8 against 22.3 at 1950). The engine computes each total as the sum of the cell value times the fraction of the cell inside the query area, with the same fractions for every variable (`engine.py`, `_agg_hyde_b7`), so it is linear and cannot create the mismatch; the totals inherit the cell-level gap. What the grazing layer contains beyond pasture and rangeland is not established (the HYDE documentation was not checked). *Prose:* state the four HYDE totals separately; never sum them or take one as the difference of the others. *Needs:* a note on what each HYDE layer contains, in the crosswalk work order.
