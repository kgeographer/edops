# Ukraine: notes toward the worked example

19 Nov 2026, from discussion between Karl and Opus. Reasoning to refer back to when worked-example design resumes. Companion to `Snyder_premises.md` and `worked-examples-predesign.md`.

## The doubt

Snyder's lecture presents Ukraine as a breadbasket central to European and world history. EDOPS cannot show that Ukraine *was* a breadbasket: that is a claim about harvests, surplus and trade over centuries, and EDOPS holds no such data.

- HYDE cropland before about 1700 is backcast from population and allocated to land judged suitable, so using it to show Ukraine was farmed is partly circular.
- BasinATLAS cropland is modern.
- Climate data is an indirect pointer.

## What EDOPS can address

The environmental preconditions the argument assumes, chiefly P5 (fertile soil, easy surplus) and P3 (rivers to the sea, so surplus can travel). These are high-persistence premises, and Snyder makes the persistence argument himself (soil fertility as "a brute fact"; the land "more or less constant"). By the method in `worked-examples-predesign.md`, modern soil, relief, river and climate data support them for early periods with little qualification. GEDA adds drought reliability from 1000 CE; 1932–33 (P15) remains the test of the no-verdict principle.

## Similarity test, 19 Nov

Climate lens (precipitation + temperature), magnitude 1.5, amplitude 0.15, shape 0.90; temperature level 3–4, range 4–6.

- Poltava: 114 basins, all in the surrounding region (Ukraine, Belarus, western Russia).
- Controls: central Iowa (76 basins, all Midwest) and central Saskatchewan (117, prairies) give the same picture. So the local cluster is how the lens behaves for continental interiors at these thresholds, not evidence that Ukraine is distinctive. Climate is strongly spatially autocorrelated; a cross-continent match needs latitude, continentality and rainfall regime together.
- Tbilisi (14, scattered), Santa Fe (8, within 313 km), Alexandria (precipitation lens, 3) fit the same reading.
- To check: Saskatchewan reports "spanning 10,188 km" though all visible matches are within about 1,500 km, so at least one matched basin is off-screen (Kazakhstan or Siberia?). And whether results are capped at 60 (counts above 60 suggest not).
- The Poltava cluster runs north into forest and forest-steppe: climate alone does not separate the black-earth steppe from the forest belt. Soil does, and no lens includes soils.

Loosening thresholds until other grain belts appear would be fitting to the motivating case. The fair form of the comparison is direct: Poltava beside Iowa, Saskatchewan, the Pampas and the Kazakh steppe, on climate and soils, values side by side, judgment left to the reader.

## Where this leaves the example

- Framed as an **audit of premises**: what an environmental instrument can and cannot contribute to a historical argument, with reasons. The premises table is its core. Not "EDOPS supports Snyder"; not to be bent toward that.
- A negative or limiting result is legitimate if the question is well posed and the reasons are clear: here, that EDOPS speaks to preconditions, not to the role the land played.
- Risks: as one of two showcase examples, a limits-only result undersells EDOPS; it needs a companion where EDOPS succeeds (Pueblo). A notebook-style example needs its own form (short narrative with key cells), and any poking should be scoped to set questions and a fixed time.
- Environmental determinism is the risk to guard against: EDOPS shows the preconditions; history did what it did with them.
