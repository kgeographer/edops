# Pueblo: notes toward the worked example

19 Nov 2026, from discussion between Karl and Opus. Reasoning to refer back to when worked-example design resumes. Companion to `worked-examples-predesign.md`.

## Why it is promising

The hypothesis names climate as a driver: drought, then failing harvests, then movement from the Four Corners (Chaco, Mesa Verde) to the Rio Grande. No direct statistics on the productivity of either region exist; climate is the inferred motivator. EDOPS speaks directly to the first link; the historian infers the rest. Other factors (conflict with neighbouring peoples, social and religious change) are outside what EDOPS can see, and the example says so.

It has a shape EDOPS handles well: two places compared over the same centuries (1100s–1200s), including the late-1200s drought.

## Validation purpose

The worked examples serve two purposes: to show how signatures are used, and to show the model corresponds to what is already known. The second requires a well-studied case: an example nobody has studied gives no basis for judging whether EDOPS's output is valid. Pueblo is a known-answer test: calibrate on an established result, then apply the instrument to less-studied cases (Ukraine's premises, Societies, African Regions).

## Which parts confirm, which parts test

- **Confirms the data is served correctly (overlapping sources):** the drought record. LBDA is built largely from the Southwest tree-ring chronologies the drought literature rests on, so EDOPS showing late-1200s drought in the Four Corners is close to guaranteed.
- **Tests the model (independent):** the water. The Rio Grande carries snowmelt from the Colorado mountains; Four Corners basins depend on local rain. EDOPS's upstream/local precipitation comparison, exogenous water flag, discharge and basin aggregation come from HydroATLAS and EDOPS's own methods, not tree rings. If the signatures show the destination buffered by water from elsewhere, that is an independent line of evidence consistent with the accepted account.

The example states which is which.

## Before it can be built

- LBDA is not ingested.
- LMR's 2° grid may not separate Chaco from Santa Fe; the cells under both basins are still to check.
- The water-routing measures for the origin and destination basins, to look at early: if they don't show the contrast, the example loses its independent part.
