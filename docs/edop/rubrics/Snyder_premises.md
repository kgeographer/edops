## The lecture's own persistence claim

Snyder grounds the deep history in geography that "doesn't change much" (52:41): after the ice age the land north of the Black Sea is "more or less constant", with "big change in the 20th century" (54:03); soil fertility is a "longue durée" factor and "a brute fact" (57:02–57:48).

That is the worked example's method stated by its source. EDOPS can test it band by band: what the modern signature shows, and which 20th-century changes (irrigation, reservoirs, ploughed steppe) appear in the data.

## Notes for the example

- **Ecoregion, not land cover.** The steppe premises (P1, P4, P8, P10) need potential vegetation. Modern land cover shows ploughed steppe; that difference is itself a 20th-century change worth showing.
- **The Dnipro is low persistence.** The modern signature includes the 20th-century reservoir cascade, which flooded the rapids that shaped Viking portage and trade (P2). EDOPS describes today's regulated river; the example states what that hides. *Check: BasinATLAS degree-of-regulation and reservoir attributes.*
- **Portage at the divides.** The Baltic–Black Sea portages lie on basin boundaries, a premise the basin unit represents directly.
- **The open-east claim spans thousands of kilometres.** A single-basin signature cannot show it. A row of basins, or the similarity lens run eastward from a Ukrainian steppe basin, could.
- **1932–33 is a test of the no-verdict principle.** GEDA can report summer soil moisture for those years in the affected basins. Snyder attributes the famine to policy; how much weather contributed is debated. EDOPS reports the values and leaves the interpretation to the historian.
- **Scope of time-specific data.** Of the fifteen premises, five fall where GEDA applies (P12–P15, and P2 in part). The rest rest on persistence, or on nothing (P6). That distribution is the honest picture for Ukraine.
