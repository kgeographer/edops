Each strip shows every society coded with the selected trait value, one tick per society, positioned by that society's own percentile on a single variable's range across all basin-joined D-PLACE societies (aridity, temperature, seasonality, ruggedness, landform position). A cluster of ticks toward one end suggests those societies tend to share that end of the variable; ticks spread evenly across the strip suggest no relationship to that particular measure.

The composition donut resolves each society's language family via Glottolog and is hover-linked to the strips and the map: hovering a family highlights only that family's societies everywhere at once. This is a quick way to check whether an apparent cluster in a strip is broadly shared across many unrelated families, or driven mostly by one or two related families sitting in the same place — shared ancestry or geographic contact, rather than an independent pull toward that environment. Families below 20 societies in the current selection are marked "small n" in the donut legend; "Unresolved" covers societies Glottolog could not match to a family.

What this does not show. This is a descriptive, exploratory view, not a statistical test — High gods (EA034) has no named theoretical hook to confirm here, unlike Dominant subsistence's Climate envelope, so a visible cluster is a pattern worth a closer look, not evidence of an environmental effect. The five variables are not independent of each other (aridity and temperature correlate globally, for instance), so a cluster on one strip often reappears on a related one without adding new information. The hover-by-family probe is a visual check, not the family-restricted permutation control used elsewhere in EDOPS's statistical work — it can suggest a pattern is really just one family's geography, but it can't rule diffusion in or out with any rigor.
