Precipitation regime and Temperature regime both rank the 254 corpus cities by a composite distance: each city's monthly rainfall or temperature pattern — magnitude, seasonal shape, how sharply it swings across the year — is reduced to a handful of numbers, standardized, and compared to the query city's own numbers. The smaller the distance, the closer the match; results are a ranked list, not a fixed set.

Terrain regime works differently. Each of three measures — mean elevation, the range of relief nearby, and where the point sits within that relief (valley floor vs. ridge) — is computed live from a small elevation grid around each city rather than read from a stored value, and compared to the query city's own measures via its own tolerance band rather than one combined score. A city only qualifies if all three measures fall within the query's bands; qualifying cities are then ranked within that eligible set.

All three predate Sandbox's own similarity panel and use this older composite-distance / tolerance-band approach rather than the all-conditions-must-hold conjunction method Sandbox uses — see the project documentation's Similarity page for the fuller comparison.
