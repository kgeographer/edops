Each of the 254 corpus cities has a Wikipedia article, and its sections are sorted into four themes — History, Environment, Culture, Modern — by their headings. All the sections belonging to one theme are condensed into a short summary of that theme alone, so each city ends up with four independent short profiles rather than one undivided article.

Each summary — plus a fifth, Composite, built by combining all four — is converted into a numerical embedding, a machine-readable representation of its meaning. Two cities are "similar" under a given band when their embeddings point in a similar direction, regardless of whether they share any actual words — Environment can surface a shared physical setting even when the two Wikipedia articles are worded nothing alike.

This measures how similarly two places are described, not any environmental or cultural fact about them directly — it inherits whatever Wikipedia happens to emphasize or omit for each place, and is a different kind of similarity than the environmental lenses above, which compare measured data rather than text.
